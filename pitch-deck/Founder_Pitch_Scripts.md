## 90-second founder pitch (~220 words)

Applying for a public service still means starting from zero. One person might use three portals, each with its own account and rules, and enter the same identity, address, and income details every time. Then they find, upload, and wait on the same documents again.

It isn't a minor inconvenience. In the final month of the recent Medicaid unwinding, about two in three people who lost coverage lost it for procedural or paperwork reasons, according to KFF.

Financial technology moved faster once developers had a simpler way to connect to banking information. Government services never got that layer.

DigiPae is building it: consent-based infrastructure that helps authorized organizations retrieve permitted government documents, run verification, and support eligibility workflows through one integration. We connect only where access is legally permitted and an integration path exists.

We're starting with the organizations that already do this work by hand: benefits-technology providers and navigation nonprofits. Then we'll expand to health centers, Medicaid plans, and community action agencies.

## 3-minute investor presentation (~450 words)

**[Cover]** I'm [name], founder of DigiPae. We're building the connectivity layer for government services.

**[Problem]** Government services are connected by paperwork, not infrastructure. A single applicant moves between portals with different accounts and eligibility rules. Identity, address, income, and household details get re-entered for every program, and documents are found, uploaded, and checked by hand. Caseworkers repeat those same steps on the other side.

**[Why it matters]** That friction has real costs. KFF found that in the final month of the Medicaid unwinding, about two-thirds of disenrollments were for procedural or paperwork reasons. GAO has documented for years that benefit programs require similar information about the same people. And OMB estimates the public spends about ten and a half billion hours a year on federal paperwork.

**[Solution]** DigiPae is one consent-based connection that serves many government workflows. On one side are authorized sources: government records where access is permitted, document issuers, and identity-verification providers. On the other side are the organizations helping people apply. In between, DigiPae handles consent, document retrieval, verification, and normalized data. Consent is necessary, but it does not grant access by itself. We only connect where access is legally permitted.

**[Product]** Today's journey is repeated forms, manual collection, verification delays, then the application. With DigiPae, the person authorizes once, permitted records are retrieved, verification is structured, and the application is prepared faster. That's the intended benefit, and we'll measure it with design partners before we claim a number.

**[Architecture]** For customers it's one integration covering consent, documents, identity verification, normalization, eligibility workflows, developer APIs, and audit and security. Some of it is prototyped, some is in development, and some is planned. The slide labels each one.

**[Go-to-market]** We start with benefits-technology providers and navigation organizations, whose first need is to stop re-collecting documents. Next come health centers, Medicaid plans, and community action agencies. We extend to adjacent workflows only where integration paths and demand exist.

**[Business model and market]** Revenue comes from usage-based APIs, platform subscriptions, and enterprise contracts. Built bottom-up from public counts, the initial segments represent about $92 million at illustrative contract values. We'll replace those assumptions with discovery data.

**[Competition]** Login.gov, ID.me, Equifax, Truv, Propel, and Plaid each solve part of this. Many are potential partners. Our focus is consent-based connectivity for government documents and eligibility, delivered as developer infrastructure.

**[Progress and team]** We have the brand, product design, and prototype, plus identity verification integration and customer discovery in progress [add numbers]. The team is [founders]. Our first hires are a founding integrations engineer and privacy and policy advisers.

**[Ask]** We're raising [amount] to sign design partners, ship our first permitted integration, and run a measured pilot. Government services should be easier to connect to, easier to navigate, and easier to build upon. Thank you.
